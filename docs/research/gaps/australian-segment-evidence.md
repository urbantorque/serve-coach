# Gap fill: which Australian segment, and the evidence for it

*Prepared 27 September 2026. Australian English; prices in AUD. Resolves the "australian-segment-evidence" gap: research 02 ranked seven segments but made no final pick, the national adult competition population was UNVERIFIED, and D8 (no account, no upload) sat awkwardly beside a coach-led launch.*

**Evidence labels** (same as research 02 and 08): [Established] peer-reviewed or strong consensus · [Coaching interpretation] expert practice, not directly proven · [Vendor claim] a company says so · [Market data] participation, prices, fees, rating counts, competition rules · [Regulation] law or platform rule · [Our inference] our reasoning or arithmetic. **UNVERIFIED** marks anything we could not confirm from a primary page. Sources are numbered [G1]–[G25] (list at the end); earlier research is cited as (research 02 [S13]).

**Method and limits.**
- We read primary PDFs for the 2024–25 annual reports of Tennis Australia, Tennis Victoria, Tennis West, Tennis SA and Tennis ACT. We also read the 2026 Tennis Victoria Pennant Rules of Play, the Tennis Victoria 2022–23 report, the Tennis Queensland 2022–23 report, the Tennis NSW 2023 report (Wayback copy), a metropolitan association report (Waverley & District, 2023–24) and the Tennis Seniors Queensland 2024 report.
- **AusPlay 2025 is available as downloadable xlsx data tables** (released 30 April 2026). We parsed the "By Sport", "National" and all eight state/territory workbooks [G1–G3]. The tables do **not** include frequency, venue or cost *by activity*; those splits exist only in the Power BI dashboard.
- **Not reachable:** the Tennis NSW 2023–24 and 2024–25, Tennis Queensland 2023–24 and 2024–25, Tennis Tasmania and Tennis NT 2024–25 reports all returned 404 (tennis.com.au has moved files and Wayback has no complete copy). Tennis Victoria's UTR Grading Guide and the SA Winter Pennant regulations returned 403/404. **Reddit** refused both direct access (HTTP 403) and our search tool ("domain not accessible"), and the PullPush mirror rate-limited us. So we have **no Reddit or forum evidence**; §4 says what that means.

## 1. Recommendation

**Primary segment (one sentence).** Rated adult club-competition players, mostly UTR ~3–7 and aged ~25–60, in Australia's inter-club, association and night/midweek leagues (TV Pennant, SA Winter Pennant, WA Tennis League, Canberra Tennis League and the metropolitan associations), who play weekly through a season and can book a court alone to practise. **Size:** about **35,000–60,000 adults** nationally play graded team competition in a year, of whom roughly **25,000–45,000** are likely to be UTR ~3–7. **Confidence:** medium that the pool is in the tens of thousands (three independent routes agree, §2.4); low on the exact bounds and on the UTR 3–7 share (no public UTR distribution, §3). [Our inference, built on [G1][G3][G4–G11][G13]]

**Launch beachhead: metropolitan Melbourne.** Victoria has the densest documented competition structure: TV Pennant has 419 teams from 135 clubs [G7] and ~9.8 rostered players per team [G8]; there are 793 affiliates [G7]; and one metropolitan association alone runs about 550–600 adult team-entries a year [G13]. Victoria also has 1,279 TA coach members (+32%) [G7] and 538,678 casual court bookings (+21%) [G7]. NSW has more organised adult players (162,600 vs 122,600 [G3]), but no public count of its competitions, so it is a second-wave market until beta data exists. [Our inference]

**Secondary segment.** Adult improvers at UTR ~1–3, or unrated, who learn in coached group programs, Cardio Tennis, and social or "flex" leagues. They are reached **through their coaches**. This group covers the brief's UTR 1–2 end, which competitions barely contain (§3). In 2025, 76,800 adults played tennis organised by an individual coach and 441,500 played any organised tennis [G1]. The secondary segment sits inside those pools; its size is not measurable from desk data. [Market data; Our inference]

**Why this segment and not the others (research 02 §9).**
- **Countable and reachable.** It is the only segment with named, countable competitions, fixed weekly match days and a rating players already see. TV Pennant sets its singles playing order by UTR, with a 0.50 variance rule [G9]. SA and WA also use UTR ordering or grading [G16] (research 02 [S16]).
- **Plays often.** Tennis West's booking data show club members averaging **14 bookings a year against 3 for casual players** [G4] [Market data]. Members and competitors have the practice opportunities a serve loop needs; casual bookers mostly do not.
- **Outcomes are public.** Serve results are visible to teammates in rubbers scored weekly. We found **no direct evidence** that these players see their serve as their main problem, so this is the claim the beta must test first (§8, §10).

### 2.4 Triangulation: how many adults play graded team competition?

We used three independent routes. All are [Our inference], and the arithmetic is shown.

1. **Ratio route.** Adult league players divided by AusPlay organised adults gives 7.6% in WA (3,429 ÷ 45,170) and 7.4–8.5% in the ACT (950–1,100 ÷ 12,893). In both places the state body runs most inter-club play. Applying 7.5–8% nationally (× 441,458) gives **~33,000–35,000**. This is a floor for states where associations and private operators run many comps outside the state body (VIC, NSW, QLD).
2. **Bottom-up route.**

   | State | Estimate | Basis |
   |---|---|---|
   | WA | 3,429 | [G4] |
   | ACT | 950–1,100 | [G6] |
   | SA | 2,000–3,500 | 296 winter teams × 6–8, plus ≥234 adult summer teams × 4–6, with overlap |
   | QLD | ~4,000–4,400 | 7,358 × WA's 55–60% adult share (2022–23, sanctioned leagues only) |
   | VIC | 10,000–25,000 | TV Pennant ≈ 3,500–4,100 + Country Week ≈ 1,400 + associations; Waverley alone ≈ 550–600 adult team-entries a year |
   | NSW | 12,000–13,000 | Ratio route |
   | TAS + NT | ~700 | Ratio route |
   | **Total** | **≈ 33,000–51,000** | Plus unlisted private night comps |

3. **Rating route.** 90,000+ active rated players nationally include juniors [G10]. If adults are 40–60% of them (WA leagues are 58% adult [G4]), the result is **~36,000–54,000** adults with an active rating.

**Result:** about **35,000–60,000 adults** a year. The upper bound allows for private and club-run night comps that no report counts. Research 02's "tens of thousands" is confirmed; the figure is **not** in the hundreds of thousands. This matters for revenue (§8.1).

**What this means.**
- TV Pennant's upper and middle **men's** grades sit at UTR ~8–10, above the brief's range [UNVERIFIED thresholds].
- Women's grades, lower men's grades, Masters doubles, association weekend comps and night and midweek comps are where UTR ~3–7 adults most plausibly play. We have no distribution to confirm this [Our inference].
- **UTR 1–2 adults are essentially absent from graded competition.** They sit in Cardio Tennis, adult group terms and social or flex leagues, and many have no rating at all [Our inference].
- So the brief's UTR 1–7 range covers two different customers:
  - **UTR ~3–7 competitors** (primary): self-directed, and motivated by match outcomes.
  - **UTR ~1–3 improvers** (secondary): coach-led, and motivated by learning to serve reliably.
- D6's cue split (analogy cues for UTR 1–3, target/outcome cues for UTR 4–7) already fits this. Onboarding should ask "Do you play in a competition?" as well as UTR, because many secondary-segment players cannot answer the UTR question.

**How many competitors are UTR 1–2 versus 3–7?** Unknown. As a planning assumption, 70–75% of competition adults are UTR ~3–7, 20–25% are above 7, and under 5% are at 1–2. This gives 25,000–45,000 at 3–7 [Our inference; low confidence]. Collect UTR, or "no rating", at beta onboarding to replace this.

## 5. The coach channel against D8

**Evidence on how Australian coaches handle student video.**
- None of the ten Australian coaching sites we scanned advertises video analysis or names a video tool. This extends research 08 §2.5, which found none of seven [Market data; small sample]. It is weak evidence: we scanned page text only, and several sites build their content with JavaScript.
- Coach-video apps show no visible Australian tennis use in their AU reviews [G19].
- TA's own Technique App has been unmaintained since 2019 [G17].
- Coach supply by state: TA 3,766 coach members [G10]; VIC 1,279 (+32%) [G7]; SA 267, of whom 140 are trainees, 69 qualified and 58 business [G5]; ACT 162 [G6] [Market data].
- Tennis Coaches Australia sells a A$50 add-on membership to TA coaches [G24]. It has no published technology partner.
- **We found no public evidence of the channel coaches actually use.** Messaging apps (WhatsApp, Messages) are the likely default, but this is **UNVERIFIED**. Five coach interviews would settle it quickly (§11).

**Could an account-free export meet coaches' needs?** [Our inference]

| Coach job | Export via the iOS share sheet (D8-compatible) | Needs accounts or upload (conflicts with D8) |
|---|---|---|
| See what the student's serve looks like | Yes: 3 key frames (setup, trophy/drop, contact) plus up to 3 clips of 6–10 s each | — |
| See the numbers behind the app's advice | Yes: one-page PDF (focus, 1–2 stats with n and labels, cue, drill) plus CSV | — |
| Reply with their own cue or drill | Yes, via a **"focus code"**: a deep link or short text code the coach sends back by message. It carries no personal data, only focus ID, cue text and drill ID | — |
| Watch progress between lessons without being sent anything | No | Yes (hosted link or coach workspace) |
| Manage many students in one place | No | Yes (coach dashboard) |
| Recommend the app and earn a referral | Yes: Apple offer codes need no in-app account (research 08 [S42]) | — |

**Verdict: D8 and a coach channel can coexist in v1**, provided "coach channel" means *coaches recommend the app, and players push evidence to them*. It cannot yet mean *coaches work inside the app*.

This requires two changes elsewhere:
- **Replace the hosted-link wording in research 08 §6.** "Share one session link" and "unlimited coach sharing (coach views free)" imply hosted pages, which D8 rules out. v1 should read "export package via the share sheet". We **flag** this rather than change DECISIONS.md.
- **Protect privacy in exports.** Exports are started by the player and exclude pain notes by default. Clips strip location metadata, and a one-line reminder about bystanders appears before sharing. This is consistent with research 07's default-off coach-sharing toggle [Our inference].

**Trigger to revisit D8 (proposal).** Consider a consented coach workspace in phase 2 if both of these hold in the beta:
- at least 30% of activated beta users export to a coach at least once; and
- at least half of interviewed coaches say one-off exports are not enough.

## 8. Strongest disconfirming evidence

1. **The segment is small.** 35,000–60,000 competition adults means that even 5% paid conversion gives about 1,750–3,000 payers. At A$77.26 net per annual plan (research 08 §3), that is **A$135k–A$230k a year**, not a scalable iOS business on its own. The pennant segment is a **beachhead**. Commercial success depends on the next ring: 197,000 club-organised and 441,000 organised adult players [G1]. [Our inference]
2. **The flagship pennant is stronger than the brief's range.** TV men's Grades 3–5 average UTR 8.2–9.5 [G15, UNVERIFIED]. "Pennant player" is not a synonym for "UTR 3–7".
3. **No voice of the customer.** No Australian review, thread or survey shows serve-specific pain. AU SwingVision reviewers talk about price, reliability and processing [G18]. AI serve apps have zero AU ratings [G17]. That could mean no demand, or simply no marketing.
4. **Paid digital coaching is rare.** 3.1% of active adults pay for online programs [G2]. Stated interest will overstate purchase.
5. **Australians are not tech-starved.** SwingVision's per-player AU footprint is at least the US level (§4.2). We must win on fit and price, not on being first.
6. **The competition pool churns.** About 40% of TV Pennant players did not return the next season [G8]. Season-shaped purchases may not renew.
7. **Incumbent alignment with TA.** SwingVision is TA's official tracking partner [G22]. A TA partnership or "UTR perk" route may be harder than research 08 §7 assumes.

**Why we still recommend it.** Every other segment is either less reachable (C, D, G), carries more regulatory risk (E, F), or is a channel rather than a payer (B) (research 02 §9.2). Weakness 1 changes the plan (beachhead, then expand), not the pick. Weaknesses 3–4 are exactly what the beta can test cheaply. [Our inference]

## 9. Implications

### 9.1 Pricing
- **Keep A$99.99 a year as the control; test A$79.99 and A$129.99** (research 08 §6). The segment's small size argues against pricing only for the most committed. If A$129.99 wins on intent, confirm it at soft launch with a live test before adopting it [Our inference].
- **Season pass: keep A$39.99, fix the duration** to season end dates (§6.1). Name it for the season ("Winter season pass, to 30 Sep"). Measure cannibalisation of the annual plan (research 08 Open question 12).
- **Plan revenue on expansion.** Model the pennant beachhead at 2,000–3,000 payers maximum. Add the secondary segment (coach-led improvers) and the wider organised pool for any figure above that [Our inference].

### 9.2 v1 features
- **Keep the player-entered match log, made lighter.**
  - After each comp match, three taps: first serves in (approximate %), double faults, and "serve felt: better / same / worse".
  - Label it `player-entered` (D2/D3). It answers "Am I getting more first serves in?" where it matters, in matches.
- **Season framing.**
  - Add "pre-season serve check", "mid-season check" and "finals check" templates.
  - Add a local notification on the player's practice day, not their comp day. No account is needed.
- **Coach export package (v1).**
  - Contents: PDF one-pager, 3 key frames, up to 3 clips, CSV, sent through the share sheet.
  - Add a "focus code" import so a coach's cue or drill can replace the app's suggestion. Record it as `coach-recommended` (D2 evidence tier).
- **Onboarding.**
  - Ask about competition status (TV Pennant / association / night or midweek / social or flex / none) and UTR (optional, with "no rating").
  - Use the answers to choose cue style (D6) and match-log visibility. Store them on the device.
- **Defer:** accounts, hosted share links, coach dashboards, captain or team features.

### 9.3 D8
- **No change to D8 is needed for v1**, with two **flags** for the product lead:
  1. Research 08 §6's "share one session link" and "coach views free" rows conflict with D8. Replace them with the export package.
  2. The fake-door test (§11) and the survey need **beta-only, opt-in measurement**. Without it, "no upload" means we cannot see paywall taps. Two options:
     - a TestFlight-only consented event log sent by the tester through the share sheet at weeks 2 and 6; or
     - a minimal anonymous endpoint shown in the beta consent screen.

     Choose one explicitly. Silent telemetry would breach D8.
- **Phase-2 trigger** for a consented coach workspace: see §5.

## 10. Beta survey (12 questions, about 4 minutes)

Run in-app at week 2 (after the first "Did it work?"), or by link for people recruited but not yet active. Keep answers anonymous and optional. Do not ask about pain here: pain stays local-only in the app (D8), and health information is sensitive under the Privacy Act (research 07).

1. **Which competitions do you play in this year?** (tick all) TV/State Pennant · Association weekend comp · Night or midweek comp · Social or flex league · Tournaments · None. **State:** ___
2. **What is your UTR?** Singles ___ · Doubles ___ · "I don't have one" · "Don't know". If none: which best describes you? (just learning to serve / can rally and play sets / play regular comp)
3. **Over your last 5 comp matches, how often did your serve cost you a game you should have won?** Never · Once or twice · Most matches · Every match · I don't play comp
4. **Rank the parts of your game you most want to improve this season** (drag to order): serve · return · forehand · backhand · net play · movement or fitness · tactics or mental. *The serve is not listed first, to avoid leading.*
5. **In a typical comp match, roughly what share of your first serves go in, and how many double faults do you hit per set?** (bands, with "no idea")
6. **In the last 4 weeks, how many times did you hit serves without a hitting partner or coach?** 0 · 1 · 2–3 · 4+. **Where?** club court · booked council court · public or free court · other
7. **Which of these have you used in the last 12 months to work on your serve?** Private lesson · group class · YouTube or online video · filmed myself on a phone · SwingVision · another app (name) · nothing
8. **Do you currently have a coach?** No · Occasionally · Regularly. **If yes: how do they see video of you?** They film in lessons · I send clips by WhatsApp/Messages · email · an app (name) · never
9. **Would you send your coach a one-page serve summary with 3 photos and short clips from this app?** Yes · Maybe · No · I don't have a coach
10. **About how much did you spend on tennis in the last 12 months (lessons, comp fees, court hire, excluding gear)?** <A$200 · A$200–499 · A$500–999 · A$1,000–2,499 · A$2,500+
11. **Van Westendorp (annual price for the full app):** at what price would it be so cheap you'd doubt it? · a bargain? · getting expensive but you'd consider it? · too expensive to consider? (four A$ fields)
12. **When does your main comp season start, and would a pre-season serve check two weeks before round 1 be useful?** Month ___ · Yes · Maybe · No

**Decision rules (set before fielding)** [Our inference]:
- If fewer than 25% of comp players rank the serve in their top two (Q4) **and** fewer than 30% answer "most/every match" (Q3), reposition the message from "fix your serve" to "serve stats for your season".
- If fewer than 20% have hit solo serves in the last 4 weeks (Q6), the loop needs a partner-friendly mode.
- If the Van Westendorp acceptable range excludes A$99.99, move the control price.

## 11. Fake-door paywall test (50–100 beta users)

| Element | Specification |
|---|---|
| **Who** | TestFlight beta, 50–100 adults. Aim for at least 60% comp players; stratify by state (VIC first) and UTR band (none/1–3, 3–5, 5–7, 7+) |
| **When** | Once, right after the user's first "Did it work?" comparison: the proven-value moment (research 08 §6). Users who never reach it see it at their third analysed session |
| **Arms** | Random 1:1:1 at install, stratified: **A$79.99/yr**, **A$99.99/yr**, **A$129.99/yr**. Every arm also shows the **Season pass, A$39.99, to the end of the current season** as the second option. Monthly is left out in the beta to keep the choice set small |
| **Screen** | Identical in all arms except the annual price. Honesty labels and the "always free" list stay visible. Trial terms read "7-day trial, then A$X/yr" as planned |
| **On tap** | Immediate disclosure: *"Paid plans aren't on sale yet, and nothing has been charged. Thanks — your choice helps us set a fair price. Beta testers keep full access."* Then one question: *"If this had been real, would you have gone ahead?"* (definitely / probably / not sure / no) |
| **On "Not now"** | One reason: price · not enough value yet · I don't pay for apps · decide after the season · other |
| **Metrics** | (1) Intent rate = taps on either plan ÷ users shown the paywall; (2) plan mix (annual vs season pass); (3) share answering "definitely"; (4) revenue-per-exposed-user proxy = Σ(price × "definitely" taps) ÷ users shown; (5) "not now" reasons |
| **Precision** | At ~33 users per arm, a 30% intent rate carries a 95% Wilson interval of about **17%–47%**; at 17 per arm, about 13%–53%. **Only differences of about 25 percentage points or more are detectable.** Treat results as directional and read them beside survey Q11 |
| **Decision rule (pre-registered)** | Choose the **highest annual price** whose intent rate is no more than 10 points below the A$99.99 arm **and** whose revenue proxy is highest. If intervals overlap heavily, keep A$99.99 and re-test at soft launch with live experiments (research 08 §8.3 sample sizes). If season-pass share is above 50% in every arm, test annual-plan framing ("covers both seasons") before launch |
| **Compliance** | TestFlight only, never production. No payment details collected and no StoreKit purchase started. Disclose immediately so there is no misleading-conduct risk (Australian Consumer Law s 18) [Regulation]. Events are recorded only through the beta-only, opt-in route chosen in §9.3 |

## 12. What desk research cannot answer

- **Whether adult competitors feel serve pain** strongly enough to change behaviour. No Australian review, thread or survey exists, and Reddit was not reachable. Survey Q3–Q5 plus 10 interviews are needed.
- **The true UTR distribution** of Australian competition adults. Neither TA nor UTR publishes it. Collect it in onboarding, or ask TA/UTR for anonymised band counts.
- **How often these players practise serves alone**, and where (Q6).
- **Real willingness to pay.** Only behaviour answers this: fake door, then live paywall.
- **How Australian coaches receive video today**, and whether one-off exports are enough. Needs 5–10 coach interviews in Melbourne.
- **NSW and Queensland competition counts** (reports unreachable), and weekly tennis frequency by state (AusPlay Power BI only).
- **Whether ~40% pennant churn** [G8] reflects dissatisfaction with results (which the product could influence) or life events (which it could not).
- **Whether the TA–SwingVision relationship** blocks or merely complicates a TA or UTR partnership.
- **Strava or Runna AU subscriber numbers.** Not disclosed publicly.
